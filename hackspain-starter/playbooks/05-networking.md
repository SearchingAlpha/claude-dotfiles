# 05 — Networking: before, during, after

Read time: 15 min. Goals this serves: future co-founders and friends in the
ecosystem, interview leads for FDE and deployment roles, and learning how
serious founders and investors think. `cheatsheets/people-card.md` is the
printable target list; `me.md` holds your intros; `/hs-contact` logs people
and drafts the follow-ups.

## Five principles

1. **Give first.** Domain knowledge, a contact, feedback, help unblocking a
   team. The ask comes later, if at all.
2. **Be specific.** One real question grounded in their public work beats
   "what do you do?" every time.
3. **Ask for LinkedIn on the spot.** "Can I send you that?" is the natural
   moment. Never "let's connect later".
4. **Follow up within 48 hours** with the specific thing you talked about.
5. **Be the person who unblocks other teams.** That is how friendships form at
   a hackathon, and it costs 30 minutes on Saturday afternoon.

## Before the event (9–17 Sep)

- Follow the judges and mentors on X and LinkedIn. Leave two or three real
  comments on things they post. No cold DMs, no pitches.
- Read the people card once a day for the last three days. Know the funds in
  one line each.
- Rehearse the four 20-second intros in `me.md` until they sound like speech.
- Prepare three **give assets**: (a) the lessons from shipping an AI agent to
  production at Amazon scale (evaluation, guardrails, cost, the boring parts);
  (b) how freight, warehouses, forecasting and exceptions actually work, which
  is directly relevant to HappyRobot, THEKER and Embat; (c) an open offer to
  help other teams with framing and pitch on Saturday.
- One LinkedIn post before the event via `/post`: that you're going, with a
  specific angle (the product-person-among-devs role, or the agent-in-prod
  lens). It gives people something to recognise you by.

### The ask ladder

Climb one step per conversation. Never skip.

1. **Curiosity.** One specific question grounded in their public work.
2. **Help.** Offer something: a domain insight, feedback on their thing, a
   contact, a hand with a bug.
3. **Follow-up.** "Can I send you X?" Get LinkedIn now.
4. **Intro or interview.** Only in the written follow-up, only if step 2
   happened. "I'd love 20 minutes to understand how your deployment team
   works."

Never: pitch a VC your job search, ask for a job at a stand, hand anyone a CV.

### Openers by type

- **VC**: "What's the most common reason you pass on an AI-agent startup at
  seed right now?" or "Which of this weekend's tracks would you fund a company
  in?"
- **Startup judge or engineer**: "What's the failure mode of agents in your
  domain that customers never anticipate?" or "What does a great first week
  look like for an FDE at [company]?"
- **Mentor-founder**: "What did you get wrong about your early market that
  you'd tell a first-time founder?"
- **Fellow builder**: "What are you building, and what's the hardest bit right
  now?" Then actually help.
- **Anyone, via the board**: "Saw you logged firstDemo at H9, what is it?"

### The live board and the feed

`hackspain watch` puts your team on the venue board; `hackspain post` puts
your one-liner and screenshots in everyone's feed. Four posts over the weekend
mean people come to you already knowing what you're building. When you meet
someone from the board, you already have the opener.

### Making friends, concretely

Saturday afternoon, while the devs build, walk the floor for 30 minutes and
offer framing or pitch help to teams that look stuck. Sit with new people at
every meal. Remember one specific thing per person and write it down the same
night with `/hs-contact`. Friends come from repeated small helps, not from a
great first conversation.

### Co-founder fit, without making it weird

Five questions that fit naturally into hackathon conversation:

1. What would you build if you had twelve months of runway?
2. What do you want to be doing in three years?
3. What's a decision you made recently with incomplete information?
4. How do you handle disagreeing with someone you respect?
5. What do you do when the plan is clearly wrong at hour 20?

And one observation that beats all five: how do they behave at hour 30, tired,
when something breaks? Keep a "people I'd build with" shortlist in
`contacts.md` with Goal set to `cofounder`.

### HappyRobot

Personal context and approach live in `me.md`. The short version: don't lead
with the interview; lead with the demo and the domain. Earn the second look.

## After the event

- **Same night**: `/hs-contact` for every person you met, while you still
  remember the specific thing.
- **48 hours**: LinkedIn connection plus a message under 300 characters that
  references the specific thing. No ask in the first message, except "here's
  the X I promised".
- **One week**: a LinkedIn post via `/post`: what you built, one honest lesson,
  the team tagged, and one or two people you genuinely learned from.
- **Two weeks**: a value-add message to your top five contacts. Something
  useful: an article that fits their thesis, a data point from your domain, a
  small artifact. Then the ask, if there is one: an intro, a 20-minute call
  about FDE work, a coffee in Madrid.
- **After that**: quarterly touch with the shortlist. Comment on their posts.
  Send them things.

### Turning a good demo into an interview

If a startup judge liked the project, the follow-up is a warm application:
"We built X for your challenge, here's the repo. I'd love 20 minutes to
understand how your deployment team works." Careers pages for all five track
startups are in playbook 03.
